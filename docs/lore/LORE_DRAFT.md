## How to read this

This is the full truth of the PROJECT NULL world, written plainly for the people making it. Players will learn it one game at a time, but it is **not a puzzle**. Every game answers its own central question clearly, in the world, by its ending. Players who read the documents get more depth; players who do not still understand what happened.

- **[canon]** means the current game already says it. New lore must never contradict it.
- **[decided]** means a choice that has been made for the series.
- Everything else is the proposed story. It is written as fact so it can be judged as a whole.

**What changed in v1.2**

- The answers to the v1.1 questions are recorded in [section 22](#22-decisions) and applied throughout.
- The deviation ending is decided: it is PROJECT NULL's second ending and the series canon. Its trigger is now one clear action instead of "any rule".
- Subjects' former names stay out of PROJECT NULL. The Night 3 document now uses roles only.
- LOG SIX is the next game, after PROJECT NULL is finished. SUBLEVEL B keeps its forced sacrifice.

**What changed in v1.1**

- Decisions instead of options: the Null has rules, Helix has a history, and every character has a name, a past and a fate.
- New: the Rules of the Null, which tie every game mechanic to the story.
- New: the Count of Five, which explains *why* the subjects come for you.
- New: the Security Station is cell 05.
- New: R. is Contractor-0317, with a full story; Ines Varga is the fifth operator.
- New: Helix's history from 1952, the other N-sites, and the eleven people lost in Incident 17-C.

## 1. The story in one page

Under an old mine in the far north lies **the Deep**: the largest known **Null**. A Null is a place where things stop happening for reasons. Close to it, people slowly stop acting on will and start acting on chance. In the end they move only when an inner roll comes up, like a die with twenty faces. Each keeps exactly one thing of who they were, and that one thing becomes their only weakness.

The **Helix Research Directorate** has studied Nulls since 1961. It learned two facts. **Nulls grow.** And **a Null goes quiet when it holds a complete set of five changed people**, each with a different anchor. Helix decided to close the Deep by building such a set on purpose. That plan is **Project NULL**.

Four of the five were easy to make. In the winter of 1997, four N-17 staff members were changed one by one: a guard, a camera analyst, an acoustician and a long-term test volunteer. They became **Subjects 01 to 04**. The fifth was harder. To hold the other four in order, the fifth anchor had to be **procedure itself**: a person who keeps the rules perfectly, night after night, until nothing else is left. Helix built an automated trainer for that, the **Diagnostic Cycle**, run by a system called **WARDEN**.

The fifth candidate was **Dr. Ines Varga**, the woman who wrote WARDEN. On 2 March 1998, one night before she would have completed the series, she refused. At 03:12 she opened every cell, sealed herself inside the Security Station (which WARDEN treats as **cell 05**) and took the eleven people on site down into **Sublevel B**, away from the subjects. None of them came back up.

Helix then hired outsiders who would not know the rules were the test. **Contractor-0117** and **Contractor-0217** were lost. **Contractor-0317, "R."**, survived seven nights by learning to stay alive *and* deliberately break procedure, so that the series never completed. Then R. went down to find Varga.

In October 1998, Helix hires **Contractor-0417: you.** R. radios you from below. Every night you survive, Varga's old seal on cell 05 weakens. **If you complete all five nights by the rules, you become Subject 05.** The set is complete, the Deep goes quiet, and Helix has its first success.

**Winning is how you lose.** That is the heart of PROJECT NULL.

## 3. The Null

### What it is

A Null is a region where **causation thins out**. Ordinary matter is unaffected: rock, steel and water behave normally. But anything that *chooses*, from animals to people, slowly loses the ability to. Choices are replaced by chance.

Helix's first description, from 1961, is still the best one: *"Inside the field, nothing decides. Things are drawn."*

A Null has no mind and no goal. It does not hate anyone. But it behaves in one consistent way that looks like a goal: **it tries to complete sets of five.** See the Count of Five, below.

### What it does to people

Change happens in four stages. Helix calls the process **drawing**.

| Stage | Name | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Drift** | Small choices go random. A person takes a different corridor for no reason, or forgets why they stood up. They feel calm, even relieved. |
| 2 | **Thinning** | Larger choices follow. Speech falls into repetition. The person sleeps less, then not at all. They become fixed on one thing: a fear, a habit, a duty. |
| 3 | **The roll** | Will is gone. The person acts only when an inner chance comes up, at a steady interval that is different for each of them. They keep the one thing they were fixed on. Helix calls that thing the **anchor**. |
| 4 | **Settling** | The body changes to fit the anchor: taller for a walker, paler for a watcher, larger for one who endures. The change stops here. A settled subject does not age, eat or sleep. |

Distance and time matter. A day on Level 2 does almost nothing. A week near Sublevel B can reach Thinning. Directly above the Deep, Helix staff have reached the roll in under forty hours.

### The pull

Helix measures how strongly the Null acts on a changed being on a scale from **0 to 20**, named the **Calloway scale** after Helix's founder. At pull *n*, a subject's inner roll succeeds *n* times in twenty. At 0 it never acts. At 20 it acts every time.

**The pull is the AI level.** It rises with closeness to the Deep and with exposure. WARDEN controls it on purpose, by opening and closing the old mine's draft dampers to Sublevel B. That is why the levels climb hour by hour and night by night: **the schedule is WARDEN's protocol**, turning the pressure up to test the operator.

### Nulls grow

Every Null grows slowly. The Deep has risen about one level per decade. The mine's original lowest levels are now inside it. In 1991 Sublevel C was lost, and Sublevel B is now its edge. Left alone, the Deep would reach the surface in about fifty years.

This is why Helix does what it does. Helix is not evil for its own sake. It is trying to stop something that grows, and it has decided some people are the price.

## 4. The Rules of the Null

Every mechanic in the game is one of these rules. Future games may add rules, but never break these.

| # | Rule | In the story | In the game |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **The Roll** | A drawn being acts only when its inner chance comes up, at its own steady interval. | The movement opportunity: a d20 against the AI level, every 4.2 to 8 seconds per subject. |
| 2 | **The Pull** | The strength of the Null on a being, 0 to 20. | The AI level and the night schedules. |
| 3 | **The Anchor** | A drawn being keeps one thing of who they were. It is the only thing that still reaches them. | Each subject's single countermeasure: light, being watched, silence, the field. |
| 4 | **The Count of Five** | A Null quiets when it holds a complete set of five anchored beings, each with a different anchor. Until then, drawn beings are restless. They search for whatever could complete the set. | The subjects roam, and move toward the station: toward you, the only candidate. |
| 5 | **The Threshold** | Drawn beings hesitate at a boundary before crossing it, as if checking the count. | The settle rule: a pause at a door or the duct before the first try. |
| 6 | **The Recoil** | A drawn being turned back by its anchor moves away quickly, without waiting for its roll to be lucky. | Retreat: effective AI at least 10 while retreating. |
| 7 | **The Dark** | Machinery noise and light mask the Deep. In darkness and quiet, its pull is felt more strongly. | During a power failure, every subject gains +2 AI. |
| 8 | **Taking** | A drawn being that reaches an unanchored person pulls them into the Null. The person does not become a subject. They are simply gone, and the set is no closer to complete. | A breach ends the night. Nothing new appears in the facility. |

**Rule 4 is the key to the whole series.** The subjects do not want to kill you. They are drawn toward the fifth place in the set, and you are sitting in it. Reaching you too early does not complete anything; it only takes you. Only a person who arrives in the fifth place *with an anchor* completes the set. WARDEN exists to give you that anchor: procedure.

## 5. Helix Research Directorate

### Origins

- **1952. Helix Instrumentation** is founded by **Dr. Edith Calloway**, a statistician, and **Arthur Penhale**, an electrical engineer. It builds hardware random-number generators for defence simulations and actuarial work. The company name comes from Calloway's favourite image: a spiral staircase, which goes up and round at once.
- **1958.** To shield its generators from cosmic-ray noise, Helix leases deep galleries in the disused **Kestrel Deep** lead mine. Generators run 600 metres underground.
- **1961. The Flat Noise Event.** The Kestrel generators begin producing output that is *too* even: every value appears exactly as often as every other, run after run. Within weeks, three miners on the maintenance crew start behaving strangely. One walks the same gallery for nine hours. Calloway goes down herself, names the effect, and writes the first report: *"Inside the field, nothing decides. Things are drawn."* The three miners become **Set Zero**, the first known drawn people. They are never recovered.
- **1963.** Helix Instrumentation creates a research arm: the **Helix Research Directorate**. Government contracts follow, along with very little oversight.

### Becoming what it is

- **1964 to 1975.** Helix finds eight more Nulls worldwide, all much smaller than the Deep, and builds a research site over each. The sites are numbered **N-01** onward.
- **1968. The Count of Five is discovered.** At **N-04**, a coastal pumping station, an accident draws five workers at once. Each settles with a different anchor, and the N-04 Null goes silent for nine years. Calloway understands what it means and spends the rest of her life arguing that it must never be done deliberately.
- **1974.** Calloway dies. Her last memo, kept in Helix's archives, reads: *"We will be tempted to close them with people. If we do, we become a Null with an office."*
- **1975. Marius Vell** becomes Director. He is the opposite of Calloway: a planner, patient and certain. He never visits a site.
- **1979. Project NULL** is founded under Vell, with one goal: to close the Deep by building a set of five.

### How Helix works

- **Sites, not names.** Helix refers to places by number and to people by role. Contractors get numbers that encode their site: **0417 is the fourth contractor at N-17.**
- **Facilities Contracting** hires outsiders for short "maintenance" jobs through layers of agencies, and never meets them in person.
- **The voice.** Helix writes politely, precisely and without feeling. "Thank you for your participation" is not irony; it is how Helix speaks.
- **What it believes.** That the Nulls will grow until they meet, and that a few people now are better than everyone later. Most Helix staff believe this sincerely.

### The N-sites

| Site | Where | Status in 1998 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N-01 | Kestrel Deep, upper galleries | Merged into N-17 in 1986. |
| N-04 | A coastal pumping station | Closed by accident in 1968. Silent for nine years, then woke again when one of its five was moved. Abandoned. |
| N-09 | A mountain observatory | Small Null, five rooms wide. Project NULL's second site. Still active. |
| N-12 | A drained reservoir | Lost in 1983. Fenced off. |
| N-17 | Kestrel Deep, new facility | The largest Null. Project NULL's main site. |

The other N numbers belong to smaller sites, failed surveys and sites that were never built.

## 6. Project NULL and WARDEN

### The plan

Project NULL had to solve one problem: **a set of five needs five different anchors, and the anchors have to stay in order.** At N-04 the set lasted nine years, then fell apart when one of the five was moved. Helix needed a fifth anchor that *keeps the other four in place*.

The answer, proposed in 1989, was **procedure**: a person whose anchor is the act of keeping a station running, its doors, lights, fields and silences. Such a being would contain the other four forever, without ever thinking about it. Helix called the role **the Operator**.

### WARDEN

You cannot draw a person toward procedure by accident. It has to be trained into them during drawing, so that procedure is the one thing left when everything else is gone. In 1994 Helix's chief systems scientist, **Dr. Ines Varga**, designed the trainer: **WARDEN**, the automated system behind the **Diagnostic Cycle**.

How the Diagnostic Cycle works:

1. **The station is placed in the Deep's pull.** The Security Station sits directly above the old main shaft. A person working there for six hours a night begins drawing within days.
2. **The other four are released.** They come to the station because of the Count of Five. The only way to survive them is to use the station's systems correctly: light, doors, silence, glances, the field. **Fear teaches procedure.**
3. **WARDEN raises the pull** each hour and each night. The more procedure the operator needs, the more of them procedure becomes.
4. **Five perfect cycles complete the series.** After five consecutive nights in which the operator survived using only correct procedure, the operator settles, with procedure as their anchor. The set is complete.
5. **WARDEN logs deviations.** If the operator survives a night but breaks procedure in a way WARDEN records as a deviation, the series restarts: "Operator status: retained."

WARDEN never lies. It leaves things out. "The operator is not under observation" is true in the narrow sense that no camera watches the station. The operator is the only thing the Diagnostic is measuring.

### What WARDEN says, and what it means

| Message | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| DIAGNOSTIC CYCLE INITIATED | Night *n* of the series has begun. |
| SUBJECT RESPONSE NOMINAL | The four are drawing toward the station as expected. |
| OPERATOR COUNT | How many people WARDEN recognises as operators, anywhere in the facility, including below. |
| CELL 05 SEAL INTEGRITY | How much of Varga's override still protects the station. See section 9. |
| OPERATOR FATIGUE DETECTED. CONTINUE | The operator is in early Thinning. Good. |
| RETAINED FOR CYCLE N+1 | The operator survived and the series continues. |
| OPERATOR STATUS: CONTAINED | The operator has settled. Subject 05 exists. |

## 7. Research Facility N-17

N-17 was built from 1984 to 1986 inside the old Kestrel Deep mine, replacing N-01. It is a vertical facility: each level sits above an older mine level that the Null has already taken.

| Level | What is there |
| --- | --- |
| Surface | Gatehouse, lift head, vehicle shed. The only way in or out is the lift. |
| Level 1 | Offices, records, living quarters for the eleven staff. |
| **Level 2** | **Containment.** The Security Station, Containment A and B, the Isolation Ward, the Acoustics Lab with Anechoic Chamber 2, the Research Hall, Cold Storage and the Central Junction. This is the level in the game. |
| **Sublevel B** | The old mine's pump galleries and WARDEN's machine room, with the draft dampers. Directly above the Deep. Access revoked since Incident 17-C. |
| Sublevel C | Lost to the Null in 1991. |
| The Deep | The Null. Nobody knows how far down it goes. |

**The Security Station is cell 05.** It was built to be the fifth cell from the start: the place the Operator would sit forever. Its doors face outward, unlike a normal cell, because the Operator keeps the others out. Its field console is calibrated to Subject 04 because the Operator was always meant to manage the Patient. Its rear door leads past the electrical room to the Sublevel B door: the shortest route to the Deep.

## 8. The subjects

All four were N-17 staff or residents. All four were changed between October 1997 and February 1998, as the first part of Project NULL's final series. Each was chosen for a strong, simple trait, which became the anchor.

**[decided]** Their former names are revealed in later games only. In PROJECT NULL the subjects are known by number, nickname and, through the documents, their former role.

### Subject 01: The Wanderer

- **Who he was:** **Tomas Brandt**, 51, N-17's senior night guard for twelve years. A kind man who never sat down on shift. He walked the corridors every hour "to make sure", because in 1986 he had once fallen asleep at a desk while a pump flooded.
- **How he was drawn:** Helix assigned him night rounds on Level 2 with every light off, for 41 nights, "to reduce power use". His rounds stopped having a route. Then they stopped having a purpose.
- **Anchor: being seen off post.** His one fear was to be caught away from where he should be. Light finds him, so light turns him back.
- **What remains:** about 2.4 metres tall, thin, always walking. He never arrives anywhere. He still carries the shape of a torch in one hand.
- **What he wants:** to finish a round he can no longer remember.

### Subject 02: The Observer

- **Who she was:** **Mira Osei**, 29, camera analyst. She watched N-17's feeds in eight-hour shifts, and was the first to notice that the subjects moved in steady, countable rhythms.
- **How she was drawn:** Helix doubled, then tripled her shifts, until she watched the feeds for 70 hours without sleep. On the last night she stopped writing reports and only watched.
- **Anchor: watching.** She became what she did. She knows when she is watched, and holds still, because a watcher being watched is the one rule she still understands. Stared at too long, she stares back, and stops holding still. Ignored, she comes looking for someone to watch her.
- **What remains:** pale, very thin, completely still while watched. "Do not attempt to interpret its posture" refers to how she stands: like someone leaning in toward a screen.
- **What she wants:** to be seen, and to see.

### Subject 03: The Echo

- **Who he was:** **Dr. Felix Aranda**, 44, acoustician, head of the Acoustics Lab. He believed the Deep makes a sound below the range of human hearing and spent years trying to record it.
- **How he was drawn:** he spent 30 hours alone in Anechoic Chamber 2, the quietest room in the facility, listening through equipment wired to Sublevel B. He came out repeating things people had said to him days earlier.
- **Anchor: sound.** He can only follow sound now, and only repeat it. Silence starves him: with nothing to hear, he gives up and leaves. Loud places pull him in.
- **What remains:** something that moves through the ventilation, never seen clearly. When you move a door, the sound comes back from the vents, slightly wrong.
- **What he wants:** to hear the sound from below one more time. He thinks the station is where it is coming from.

### Subject 04: The Patient

- **Who he was:** **Jonah Pell**, 35, the only long-term resident of the Isolation Ward. A volunteer test subject who signed up in 1995 for "exposure tolerance trials" in return for paying off his family's debts. He was promised eighteen months. He stayed three years.
- **How he was drawn:** slowly, by design. Helix used him to learn how much exposure a person could take, and he took all of it, without complaint, because he had promised.
- **Anchor: endurance.** He does not stop. Doors are something to wait out and lean on until they give. The only thing that reaches him is the containment field, which Helix built in 1996 as a restraint for him in particular, tuned to his biometrics from three years of medical records.
- **What remains:** huge and slow, every step heavy. His breathing is the clearest sound in the facility.
- **What he wants:** to be let out, as he was promised.

### The fifth place

Subject 05 was always meant to be the Operator. Section 9 explains who was supposed to fill it, and who almost does.

## 9. Ines Varga and cell 05

**Dr. Ines Varga** (born 1957) joined Helix in 1983 as a systems scientist and wrote WARDEN in 1994. She believed in Project NULL: she had seen the projections of the Deep reaching the surface. When Helix needed a fifth candidate, she volunteered, because she did not think it was fair to ask anyone else.

She ran the series on herself from 26 February 1998: four perfect nights. On the fourth night, she realised what the Operator would actually be. Not a guardian, but a person reduced to a routine forever, still aware enough to know it. She also understood that four of her colleagues had already been turned into something like that, and that she had designed the system that did it.

On the fifth night, **2 March 1998**, at **03:12**, she refused:

1. She used her WARDEN credentials to **release all five cell locks**, including the station's. The four subjects were drawn at once toward the fifth place, which was now empty.
2. At 03:15 she **sealed the Security Station from the inside** with a personal override, so that nobody could be made into the fifth while she was gone. WARDEN recorded this as "cell 05 sealed from the inside".
3. She went through the rear door to Sublevel B, taking the other ten people on site with her, because the subjects would not follow them toward the Deep. At 03:40 WARDEN, following procedure, **revoked Sublevel B access** to protect the containment level, and locked them below.

**Cell 05's seal integrity** in the game is what is left of Varga's override. Every night a contractor completes, WARDEN erodes it: 98%, 71%, 40%, 12%. When it reaches zero at the end of Night 5, nothing protects the station any more, and the operator in it is contained.

## 11. R.: Contractor-0317

**Ruth Calder**, 33, a former merchant-navy radio officer, took the N-17 contract because it paid three months' wages for a week.

**Nights 1 and 2.** She survived by luck and fast hands, and started writing everything down: which button did what, which subject hated what.

**Night 3.** She noticed the subjects moved in rhythm and started timing them. She wrote the odds on the whiteboard in the Research Hall: "1 in 20 at midnight. 7 in 20 by five." She understood that the subjects were not hunting so much as being *pulled*.

**Night 4.** She found WARDEN's operator log through the workstation's service menu and saw her own name under SUBJECT 05 (CANDIDATE). She understood what the Diagnostic was for: *"They call it a diagnostic. I think the diagnostic is us."*

**Night 5.** She survived, but just before 6 AM she deliberately walked out of the station and back. WARDEN logged an operator deviation, and her series restarted: **"retained"**, not "contained". She had found the one way to survive and stay herself: **never complete five perfect nights.** Survive every night, but break procedure once in each series of five.

**Nights 6 and 7.** She did it again. But the drawing was working on her anyway, deviations or not. She was sleeping less. She was counting everything. She heard the knocking every night and was sure it wanted her to open the door. She wrote the strip of tape ("It isn't the fifth one. It isn't anyone.") to stop herself.

**17 March.** She recorded the tape ("Log... six? Seven. I've stopped counting the nights."), wrote the last maintenance entry, and left the note above the desk for whoever came next. Then she opened the Sublevel B door with a code she had found in Varga's files, and went down. She left the door's access revoked behind her, so the next contractor would not follow.

**In October**, from the radio relay in WARDEN's machine room in Sublevel B, she talks you through your first night. She can see the cell locks release because the machine room shows them. **"I have to go"** means she cannot stay on the radio for long: down there, the Deep is close, and she is drawing faster than you are.

**What R. wants:** to get you through the nights *without* completing the series, and to find Varga. She does not tell you about the deviation trick on the radio, because WARDEN logs the radio channel. She hid it in the documents instead; see section 18.

## 12. Incident 17-C

## 14. Sublevel B and the knocking

### Sublevel B

The old pump galleries of the mine: long, wet, and dark except for WARDEN's machine room, which still has power. The draft dampers that control the pull are there. Below the galleries, the Deep begins: not a hole, but a place where the tunnels keep going and stop making sense.

### The eleven

The people Varga led down were not drawn into subjects. They were too close to the Deep, too fast, with nothing to hold on to. **They were taken** (Rule 8), all except Varga, who had spent years near the Deep and had four nights of procedure in her. She drew more slowly. In October she is still partly herself, deep in Thinning, in the machine room with R.

### The knocking

**The knocking is not a person.** When the Null takes people, it keeps an echo of the last thing they did together. For the eleven, that was knocking on the Sublevel B door at about 03:24 on the night of Incident 17-C, waiting for Varga to open the lock. Every night, at that time, the knock repeats. It is the last moment they were all still on this side of the door.

R.'s tape note was right: "It isn't the fifth one. It isn't anyone." But R. was wrong about one thing: there *was* someone down there. Varga.

### What this means for players

- The knock is the past repeating, not a threat. It never gets louder and never comes closer.
- The "request to open" on Night 3 is a real person, R., and the game should make that clear by the end of the series (see section 17).

## 16. Endings

### The contained ending [canon]: the default

You complete five nights by the rules. Varga's seal reaches zero. "Subjects accounted for: 5 / 5. Operator status: contained. Thank you for your participation."

**What it means:** you are Subject 05. You stay at the desk. You keep the doors, the lights, the silences and the field, forever, without thinking. The four are held in order. The Deep goes quiet for the first time in its history. Helix has proven Project NULL works, and will try it at N-09.

This is the default ending, and it is a tragedy that looks like a victory.

### The deviation ending [decided]: the second ending, and canon

R.'s trick, explained plainly in the Night 5 documents. On Night 5, between 5:00 and 5:59, leave the station, go through the maintenance corridor to the Sublevel B door and **hold `E` on it for a few seconds**, then get back and survive until 6 AM. It is the one thing procedure forbids most: the work order says to remain in or near the station. WARDEN logs an operator deviation. At 6 AM: "Operator status: **retained**. Series restarted." R. comes on the radio, for the first time since Night 1: "You found it. Good. Now come down."

**What it means:** you survived and stayed yourself, but Varga's seal is gone and WARDEN will try again. This ending leads into the sequel.

**How it plays.** The trip takes about 20 seconds there and back, while all four subjects are at their Night 5 peak and nobody is at the desk. Players choose when to go. The best moment is right after the Patient has been sent home and the Echo has left the vents.

### Which is canon for the series? [decided]

**The deviation ending.** Contractor-0417 goes down to Sublevel B. The contained ending is what happens to a player who never questions the rules, and the sequel can mention it as what WARDEN expected.

## 17. How the story is told

The rule for every game: **one central question, one clear answer, told in the world.** No hidden minigames, no ciphers needed to understand the plot, no answers spread over five games.

| Game | Central question | The answer, told plainly by the end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **PROJECT NULL** (this game) | What is happening to me? | You are being made into Subject 05. The cell is the station. Winning by the rules completes it; breaking one rule on the last night keeps you yourself. |
| **LOG SIX** (prequel) | Who was R., and how did she survive? | Ruth Calder found the deviation trick, and went down to find the woman who refused. |
| **SUBLEVEL B** (sequel) | What is down there? | The Null, Varga, R., and the echo of the eleven. And a way to close the Deep without a fifth person. |
| **N-09** (side story) | Is it only N-17? | No. Helix is running Project NULL at a second site, using the method proven at N-17. |
| **THE DIRECTORATE** (spin-off) | Why does Helix do this? | Because it believes the Nulls will reach everyone, and because one man stopped seeing people as people. |

Depth is optional; understanding is not. Documents give names, dates and feelings. The plot itself is always told through things every player sees: the terminal, the radio, the endings.

## 19. Future games

Every game keeps the series' promises: transparent rule-driven AI that players can learn, honest mechanics explained in the world, and no jumpscares.

### PROJECT NULL: LOG SIX (prequel)

**[decided]** The next game, to be started once PROJECT NULL is finished.

- **You play:** Ruth Calder, Contractor-0317, March 1998.
- **Structure:** seven nights. Nights 1 to 3 are like PROJECT NULL, but with no guide, no radio and no How It Works. The whiteboard in the Research Hall is the player's notebook: the game fills it in as Ruth works out each rule.
- **New mechanic:** WARDEN's operator log. From Night 4 the player can read their own Diagnostic score and learn what a deviation is.
- **Story beats:** Ruth finds the subject files. She hears the knock. She writes the strip of tape. Night 5 is the first deviation ending. Nights 6 and 7 get harder because Ruth is drawing: her clock occasionally skips, and her own hands sometimes fail to respond on the first press, a roll of her own.
- **Ending:** 17 March. Ruth records the tape, writes the note, and opens the Sublevel B door. This ties directly into PROJECT NULL.

### PROJECT NULL: SUBLEVEL B (sequel)

- **You play:** Contractor-0417, after the deviation ending.
- **Setting:** the pump galleries, WARDEN's machine room, and the edge of the Deep.
- **New mechanic: you roll too.** Below, you are drawing. Every action you take has your own pull: a chance to fail, shown openly on screen. You lower it with your own anchors: light (a lamp with limited oil), sound (a radio playing R.'s voice), and attention (keeping a written log). The game is about keeping yourself together while moving through a place where things are drawn.
- **Story:** find R. and Varga in the machine room. Learn that the Count of Five can be satisfied *without* a person: Varga's plan is to make WARDEN itself the fifth anchor, a procedure with no one inside it. To do it, someone has to carry WARDEN's core down to the Deep and stay with it until it settles.
- **Ending [decided]:** the player **has to** choose who stays: R., Varga, or you. There is no way out without a sacrifice. Each choice is clear and final, and the Deep closes either way.

### PROJECT NULL: N-09 (side story)

- **You play:** a new contractor, Contractor-0109, at the N-09 mountain observatory in 1999, after Helix's success at N-17.
- **New subjects** with new anchors, for example:
  - a subject that only moves in darkness and is anchored to starlight through the observatory dome
  - a subject that copies the player's last action
  - a subject that only moves while the player moves
- **Story:** the N-09 staff know exactly what WARDEN does, and some of them want you to complete the series, because it would save them.

### THE DIRECTORATE (investigation spin-off)

- **You play:** a Helix records analyst in 2003, reviewing recorded nights from N-17 and N-09. No creatures.
- **Mechanic:** replay recorded nights deterministically (the engine already can), pause, rewind, and read every roll. The task is to find the moment each operator broke, or deviated.
- **Story:** Marius Vell's files, Calloway's warning, and the analyst slowly noticing that their own work schedule matches WARDEN's protocol.
- **Ending:** a work order arrives, addressed to the analyst, with a site number.
