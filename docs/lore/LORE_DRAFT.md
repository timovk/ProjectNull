# PROJECT NULL: lore draft

**Status:** first draft, for discussion. Nothing here is in the game yet, apart from the facts in the next section.

This document does three things:

1. It collects everything the current game already says, so new lore never contradicts it.
2. It proposes the story behind it: what Helix is, what Project NULL is, what the subjects are, and who you are.
3. It sketches how that story could carry further games.

Anything marked **[canon]** is already in the game. Anything marked **[proposal]** is new and open to change. Questions that need your decision are collected at the end.

---

## 1. What the game already establishes [canon]

These facts come from the in-game documents, the tape, the radio, the terminal and the endings (`src/content/`).

**The organisation and the place**

- **Helix Research Directorate** runs the site through its Facilities Contracting branch.
- **Research Facility N-17.** You work on **Level 2**, in the Security Station. Below it is **Sublevel B**, whose access was revoked by facility automation.
- The facility is officially **unoccupied**. Movement on camera is to be logged as a "sensor artifact".

**You**

- You are **CONTRACTOR-0417**, a Contract Systems Technician, hired by work order 4471-N17.
- Your job is to "supervise the automated Diagnostic Cycle that reactivates the monitoring infrastructure", from 00:00 to 06:00.
- Each morning the terminal marks you **RETAINED** for the next cycle.

**R.**

- R. is the previous technician. R. left a handwritten note above the desk, radios you on Night 1, and recorded the tape in the electrical room.
- On the tape, R. has "stopped counting the nights" (six or seven), believes "the diagnostic is us", and has noticed that the subjects "move when the numbers say so". R. has worked out the odds ("one in twenty early on, by five it's seven in twenty") and written them on a board.
- R.'s last radio message: "Six o'clock comes. It always does. ...I have to go."

**Incident 17-C**

- At 03:12, all five cell locks released at once, with no operator input.
- Subjects 01 to 04 were seen outside containment. Subject 05 was never seen, and **cell 05 was found sealed from the inside**.
- Sublevel B access was revoked at 03:40.
- **0 of 11** personnel were accounted for by 06:00.
- Helix's recommendation was to "reactivate monitoring via contractor" and not to brief the contractor. Subject 05's designation was later retracted.

**The subjects**

| Subject | Name | Home | Known behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | The Wanderer | Containment A | Tall. Wanders without pattern. Hates light. |
| 02 | The Observer | Containment B | Pale. Holds still while watched, grows agitated the longer it is watched, and grows bold when ignored. "Do not attempt to interpret its posture." |
| 03 | The Echo | Acoustics Lab (anechoic chamber 2) | Follows sound through the ventilation and repeats what it hears. Learns to ignore the diagnostic ping: "I think it knows." |
| 04 | The Patient | Isolation Ward | Huge and slow. Forces doors. Only the containment field, calibrated to its biometric signature, turns it back. |
| 05 | Retracted | Cell 05 | Never seen. Cell sealed from the inside. |

**The nights**

- Cell 05's seal fails a little more each night: 98%, then 71%, 40% and 12%.
- The **operator count** is 2 on Nights 1 and 2, and **3** on Night 4.
- Night 1: "Motion in sectors marked inactive", and "The operator is not under observation".
- Night 3: "Request from Sublevel B: OPEN". The request is denied and then repeated.
- Night 5: "All subjects accounted for except one", and "Please remain at your station. We are almost done."
- Ending of Nights 1 to 4: "Subjects accounted for: 4 / 5. Operator status: retained for cycle N+1."
- **Ending of Night 5:** "Diagnostic series complete. Subjects accounted for: **5 / 5**. Operator status: **contained**. Thank you for your participation."
- A taped note says: "DON'T OPEN IT. Whatever you hear down there. It isn't the fifth one. It isn't anyone."
- From Sublevel B there is an occasional knock.

The game already implies the main twist: **the fifth subject is the operator.** This draft builds on that.

## 2. The core idea [proposal]

### The Null

Deep under N-17, below Sublevel B, Helix found **the Null**: a place where cause and effect have stopped working the normal way. Nothing in the Null *decides* to happen. Things happen when the numbers come up.

People who spend too long near it change. They stop acting on will and start acting on chance. They become what Helix calls **stochastic subjects**: beings that only move when an internal roll comes up, more and more often the deeper the Null's pull.

This makes the game's mechanic part of the story:

- **The d20 is real.** Every few seconds, each subject rolls. R. was right: they move when the numbers say so.
- **The AI level is the Null's pull** on that subject. The Diagnostic Cycle turns the pull up hour by hour and night by night.
- **The countermeasures are the Null's leftover rules.** Each subject kept one piece of who it was: a fear, a habit, a sense. Light, a watching eye, silence and a calibrated field work because they press on that one remaining human thing.

### Project NULL

Helix did not set out to contain the Null. **It set out to use it.**

Project NULL's aim was an operator who never makes a mistake: someone with no panic, no fatigue and no will of their own, who simply follows procedure. Helix's theory was that the Null could produce one, by stripping a person down until only the rules remained.

The method was the Diagnostic Cycle. It is not a test of the cameras or the doors. **It is a test of the operator.** Every night it raises the pressure and measures how well the person in the chair keeps to the rules. "Subject response nominal" is about the creatures; "operator fatigue detected, continue" is about you.

Subjects 01 to 04 are Helix's **failed operators**. Each one broke in a different way, and the Null kept the shape of that failure:

| Subject | Who they were [proposal] | How they failed | What remains, and why it works |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01, the Wanderer | A night guard | Left the post to walk the corridors. "Just checking." | Still walks, never arrives anywhere. Shrinks from light: being seen off-post was what they feared. |
| 02, the Observer | The camera analyst | Watched the feeds for nights on end without sleep | Becomes what it did: it watches, and it knows when it is watched. Staring at it is staring back. |
| 03, the Echo | The acoustics researcher in anechoic chamber 2 | Spent too long in perfect silence, listening for something below | Can only follow sound now, and repeats whatever it hears. It cannot bear silence, so it leaves. |
| 04, the Patient | A patient in the Isolation Ward: a volunteer test subject | Endured everything the program did, and waited | Pure endurance. Doors are just something to wait out. The field was built to its biometrics because Helix had them on file. |
| 05 | The next operator | Has not failed yet | The cell is sealed from the inside because it is waiting for its occupant. |

### Who you are

Contractor-0417 was never hired to watch the monitoring systems. **You are the fifth subject.**

- **Cell 05's seal.** It is not keeping something in. It measures how much of you is still yours. Each night you hold to procedure, it drops.
- **Operator count.** The terminal counts the people "operating" the station. **2** is you and R. **3**, on Night 4, is you, R., and whatever you are becoming.
- **Night 5 ending.** "Subjects accounted for: 5 / 5. Operator status: contained." By surviving five nights of perfect procedure, you completed Project NULL. The perfect operator is a person who has become a rule.

That leaves the player with the central irony of the series: **winning is how you lose.** Every habit that keeps you alive is the Diagnostic working on you.

### R.

R. was the previous contractor. R. survived six or seven nights, longer than anyone, and worked out the odds.

R. understood what the cycle was doing, so on the last night R. did the one thing the procedure forbids: R. went down. "I have to go" is not R. leaving the facility. It is R. heading for Sublevel B.

The radio still reaches you from somewhere in the building, which is why R. can see the cells unlock at the moment they do.

R.'s fate stays open; see the questions at the end. The strongest version is that R. is what knocks in Sublevel B, and the "request to open" on Night 3 is R. asking to be let back up.

### Sublevel B

Sublevel B is the edge of the Null. The 11 missing personnel went down during Incident 17-C. The knock and the "request to open" come from there.

The taped note ("It isn't the fifth one. It isn't anyone.") was left by someone who heard the knock and almost opened the door. It warns that whatever asks to be let up is not a person any more, and is not a subject either. It is the Null wearing a voice.

### Incident 17-C, retold

| Time | What happened [proposal] |
| --- | --- |
| Weeks before | Project NULL's first full Diagnostic series runs on Helix's own staff. Four operators fail and become Subjects 01 to 04. |
| 03:12 | The fifth operator is about to complete the cycle. At the last moment they refuse, and open every lock. |
| 03:15 | Cell 05 seals from the inside: the cycle has no fifth subject and waits for one. |
| 03:40 | The staff flee into Sublevel B, the only place the subjects will not follow. Automation revokes access behind them. |
| 06:00 | 0 of 11 accounted for. Helix decides the cycle must be finished, with an outsider: a contractor who will not know the rules are the test. |

The fifth operator, the one who refused, is a good candidate for R.

## 3. The world [proposal]

### Helix Research Directorate

A private research directorate with government contracts and very little oversight. It thinks in sites and numbers rather than names.

- **The Facilities Contracting branch** hires outsiders for short "maintenance" jobs and never meets them.
- **Sites carry letters and numbers.** "N" sites belong to Project NULL. **N-17** is the seventeenth: there were sixteen before it, and possibly more after.
- **Paperwork voice:** dry, precise, polite and inhuman. "Thank you for your participation."

### The Diagnostic

The facility automation is not a person, but it is the closest thing the game has to a villain. It speaks only in terminal capitals, follows its procedure perfectly, and never lies; it just leaves things out.

In the voice recordings it is the FACILITY announcer. A proposal for later games: give the system a name in documents (for example **WARDEN**), but never in its own voice.

### Time and place

The tape recorder, CRT feeds, radio and paper work orders suggest the **late 1990s**, which keeps the technology simple and plausibly analogue. The location stays unnamed: somewhere cold, remote and underground.

### Tone rules for all future lore

- **Dread, not shocks.** No jumpscares, ever. The fear comes from rules, odds and what the documents leave out. This is a promise to players, not just a style.
- **The rules are honest.** Every mechanic is explained in the world (the notes, the tape, the memos) and in How It Works. The lore never contradicts the maths.
- **Answers come in pieces.** No document explains everything. Each one should answer one question and raise another.
- **People, not monsters.** The subjects were people. The horror is what was done to them, and that you are next.
- **Helix is never cartoonish.** It is calm, procedural and plausible.

## 4. Bringing more lore into PROJECT NULL [proposal]

Ideas for the current game, roughly from least to most work. Nothing here is built yet.

1. **One new document per night.** Found at the desk or in the electrical room after Night 1. For example:
   - Night 2: R.'s board, a photo of the odds table written on a whiteboard.
   - Night 3: a Helix personnel list of 11 names, four of them struck through with subject numbers written beside them.
   - Night 4: a Project NULL memo about "operator conditioning".
   - Night 5: work order 4471-N17 again, now with a field you never saw: "Candidate: 05".
2. **More tapes.** A second tape by R. ("Log nine") hidden in a different place each night, and a tape from one of the original eleven.
3. **Subject files.** A locked SUBJECTS tab on the workstation, where each file unlocks after you survive a night with that subject active. Each file gives the former role and the failure.
4. **Night 5 epilogue.** After "Operator status: contained", a slow final shot of the camera list with a 13th feed, CAM 13: Cell 05, showing the empty desk chair. No sound and no movement: just the realisation.
5. **The knock as a puzzle.** The Sublevel B knock follows a pattern, for example the number of the current night. Players who count it find a hidden note or ending.
6. **A refusal ending.** On Night 5, an operator who deliberately breaks procedure at the right moment, such as walking away from the station to the Sublevel B door at 5:59, gets a different ending: R.'s ending. This turns the series' irony into a choice.

## 5. Future games [proposal]

Each idea keeps the series' core promise: **transparent, rule-driven AI that the player can learn**, with no jumpscares.

### PROJECT NULL: LOG SIX (prequel)

- **You play R.** on nights 1 to 7 of R.'s contract, before the game you have just played.
- **New mechanic:** R. has no guide and no radio. The player has to discover the rules by experimenting, logging observations on the whiteboard. The board is the in-game How It Works, filled in by the player.
- **Ending:** the night R. decides to go down.

### PROJECT NULL: SUBLEVEL B (sequel)

- **You go down.** As 0417 after the refusal ending, or as a new character sent to retrieve the eleven.
- **New mechanic:** the roles are reversed. In the Null, **you** move on probability too. Each step is a roll, and your "AI level" is your own pull toward the Null. You manage it with light, sound and attention: the same tools, turned inward.
- **The knocking finally answered:** what asked to be let up.

### N-09 (side story)

- **Another Project NULL site**, an older and smaller one, with four new subjects and new failure modes. For example: a subject that only moves in the dark, or one that copies the player's last action.
- **Purpose:** a new game with the same engine, showing that Project NULL ran for years. Helix's paperwork links the sites.

### THE DIRECTORATE (investigation spin-off)

- **You are a Helix analyst** reviewing N-17's recordings after the fact, with no creatures to survive.
- **Mechanic:** replay recorded nights (the game already records and replays them deterministically) and find the moment each operator broke.
- **Ending:** the analyst's own work order arrives, with a site number.

## 7. Questions for you

1. **How explicit should the twist be?** Should the game ever *say* that the operator is Subject 05, or only leave it for players to piece together, as it does now?
2. **Is the Null supernatural or scientific?** This draft leans "unexplained anomaly treated scientifically". A more supernatural version (something that wants things) or a harder science-fiction version (a physics accident) would change the sequels.
3. **What happened to R.?** Down in Sublevel B and knocking, a subject already, or still somewhere in the building? It decides what the prequel and the sequel are about.
4. **Should the refusal ending exist?** It gives players a choice and a second ending, but it softens "winning is how you lose".
5. **Names.** Should R. and the original eleven get full names, or stay initials and roles to keep Helix's impersonal voice?
6. **Setting.** Is the late 1990s right, and should the location stay unnamed?
7. **Which future game interests you most**, so the lore can be deepened in that direction first?
